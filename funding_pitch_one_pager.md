# A New Data Layer for the Nonprofit Sector

The 990 is the de facto foundation for how the sector understands itself — for discoverability, transparency, research, and categorization. It was never designed for any of those purposes. It's a tax form, built for regulators, filled out by organizations that bear the cost while funders and researchers capture the value. The result is a sector that's systematically difficult to understand, fund, and support at scale: data delayed by 18+ months, inconsistent across form types, and increasingly mismatched to the questions that matter most.

Efforts to fix this have failed in a predictable pattern. Either they require all parties to align on a common standard before delivering value to anyone, or they build infrastructure that benefits consumers rather than the organizations being described — creating chokepoints whose long-term health depends on a single actor's business model.

## The insight

Agentic AI tools have independently converged on a solution to an analogous problem. Files like `CLAUDE.md` — plain text, human-authored but machine-consumable, maintained by the entity being described — have become a de facto standard for giving AI systems the context they need. They persist because the entity maintaining the file benefits directly from keeping it current.

The same logic applies to nonprofits. `organization.md` is a standardized but flexible self-description document, maintained by each organization, designed to make them more legible to funders, partners, and data systems while retaining full agency over how their work and data is presented to the world. This includes structured fields for queryable data combined with flexible prose sections for mission, theory of change, and programs. Drafts can be auto-generated from existing sources — 990 data, platform profiles on TechSoup, Charity Navigator, and elsewhere — to lower the barrier to entry. They're kept current because better legibility translates directly into better access to funding, benchmarks, and partnerships.

## The ecosystem

Files live at predictable URLs, discoverable by any platform or researcher without a central gatekeeper. Existing ecosystem actors that already verify nonprofit data for their own purposes can vouch for what they know, creating a web of trust that transfers across platforms. LLMs handle translation between an organization's file and the specific formats that grant applications, research queries, and reporting systems require — delivering on the type of "common application"-like goal the sector has pursued for years, without the alignment costs that have posed challenges in the past.

Because the standard is an open public good with no single entity controlling the data flow, its long-term health isn't tied to any platform's business model. And because it isn't US-specific, it can serve the global nonprofit sector — not just the subset that files a 990.

As AI systems increasingly mediate discovery, research, and funding decisions, nonprofits that aren't machine-readable risk becoming invisible to the tools that matter. `organization.md` is infrastructure for nonprofit participation in that world.

## Where things stand

This work is housed within the Digital Public Goods Network, a consortium of nonprofit infrastructure actors already working on related problems. TechSoup, GivingTuesday, and Charity Navigator are among the organizations that have expressed interest in participating as founding partners.

We're at the stage where founding funder and partner conversations can shape what gets built first. We'd welcome the chance to explore that with you.
